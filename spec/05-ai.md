# 05 — AI

This chapter describes how each non-player entity thinks. Each AI selects a
*mission state*; the mission state selects a *speed table* (from
`speed-tables.yaml`) and a *target reference*. The AI then steers toward the
target at the table-prescribed speed.

## Worker AI

Workers are the crystal economy. Their decisions are simple, slow-cadence,
and probabilistic.

### Mission states

| state               | meaning                                                        |
|--------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|
| `DRIFT`             | Loose patrol, no target                                        |
| `TAIL`              | Follow another entity at a small orbit (`drift_orbit_radius`)  |
| `INTERCEPT`         | Move toward a target (planetoid, crystal, or assembly point)   |
| `MINE`              | Stand off at `mining_distance` from a planetoid; extract crystal |
| `DELIVER_CRYSTAL`   | Carrying a crystal; head to Sinistar mouth                     |
| `EVADE`             | Move away from threats                                         |

### Decision cadence

Workers re-evaluate mission state every ~16 frames (not every frame). Per
evaluation:

```
if carrying_crystal:
    mission = DELIVER_CRYSTAL
elif near_free_crystal:
    mission = INTERCEPT(crystal)
elif rand() < tunables.worker.mining_probability:   # 176/255 per second
    target = pick_random_planetoid()
    mission = INTERCEPT(target) → MINE when close
else:
    mission = DRIFT
```

`tunables.yaml#mining_probability = 176/255 ≈ 0.69 per second` is the bias
toward productive behavior — workers don't sit idle.

### Crystal handoffs

- **On planetoid:** at distance `tunables.yaml#mining_distance = 32 cells`
  the worker stops, contributes vibration to the planetoid, waits for a
  toss, and grabs the crystal (probability per frame = `crystal_toss_probability` from the planetoid's side).
- **In flight, on-screen:** if a worker is within range of a free-floating
  crystal it intercepts.
- **In flight, off-screen:** worker grabs the crystal probabilistically
  (`tunables.yaml#crystal_catch_probability = 60/100 per second`) within
  `crystal_catch_distance = 12 cells`.

### Delivery

A worker carrying a crystal:

- Switches to `worker_with_crystal` entity (mass = 2, slightly heavier).
- Uses `speed-tables.yaml#stbl_worker_heavy`.
- Orbits Sinistar at `tunables.yaml#sinistar_delivery_orbit = 32 cells`
  before approaching the mouth.
- Within `tunables.yaml#crystal_delivery_tolerance = 4 pixels` of the mouth,
  delivers the crystal — Sinistar's assembly progresses by 1, the worker
  reverts to `DRIFT`, and `event_id: worker_deliver_crystal` fires.

### Speed tables

| state                  | table                              |
|------------------------|------------------------------------|
| DRIFT, TAIL            | `stbl_worker`                      |
| INTERCEPT (no crystal) | `stbl_worker_intercept`            |
| DELIVER_CRYSTAL        | `stbl_worker_heavy`                |

## Warrior AI

Warriors are pure threat. They fly in formations, fire shots, and harass.

### Mission states

| state         | meaning                                                              |
|---------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| `DRIFT`       | Loose patrol                                                         |
| `ATTACK`      | Squadron flying toward player; fly-past or intercept                 |
| `INTERCEPT`   | Solo intercept on player heading                                     |
| `MINE`        | Same as workers — stand off at mining distance, extract              |
| `TAIL`        | Follow a leader in formation                                         |
| `GUARD`       | Hold an orbital position                                             |

### Squadron formation

When 2–5 warriors form a squadron, the leader uses `stbl_warrior_leader` and
followers offset their orbit positions by the formation angles in
`tunables.yaml#formation_angle_2_ship` … `#formation_angle_5_ship`. Angles
are in CIRCLE=256 units (so 128 = 180°, 192 = 270°).

For example, a 3-ship squadron places follower 1 at +180° from the leader
and follower 2 at +270°. The geometry produces a recognizable wedge that
sweeps onto the player.

### Shooting

Warriors fire `warrior_shot` projectiles at the player. The shot is launched
in the direction from warrior to player (lead-aimed in the original, simple
direct-aim is acceptable for a remake).

Each warrior has a cooldown (`tunables.yaml#shooting_inhibitor_max = 15
frames`) **that only counts down while the warrior is on-screen and within
scroll bounds**. This prevents off-screen warriors from accumulating shots
they fire the moment they appear.

### Plan selection

The warrior plan (which speed table to use) is chosen from a per-frame
decision in `WITT/AIMWARR.ASM` based on:

- Formation role (leader vs follower)
- Distance to player
- Player heading (intercept vs fly-past)
- Squadron size

The plan-to-table mapping:

| plan                     | table                          |
|--------------------------|--------------------------------|
| Leader, fly-past         | `stbl_warrior_leader`          |
| Follower, fly-past       | `stbl_warrior_flypast`         |
| Solo intercept           | `stbl_warrior_intercept`       |
| Mining                   | `stbl_warrior_miner`           |
| Guard / orbit-defend     | `stbl_warrior_guard`           |

## Sinistar AI

Sinistar's "AI" is simpler than workers or warriors: it has one job — chase
the player — but the *feel* is what matters.

### Assembly

While `assembling`:

- Sinistar is visible but partial. The art shows progressively more pieces
  attached (worker deliveries fill in skull pieces).
- Sinistar does not pursue. It hovers near the assembly point.
- Speech: occasional `i_am_sinistar` announcements during attract; transitions
  to `beware_i_live` on full assembly.
- Hitbox is partial (only the assembled portion is solid).

When all `assembly_pieces` (originally 20, verify against ROM) have been
delivered, Sinistar transitions to `alive`.

### Alive (chasing)

Once alive:

- Mission: chase player.
- Speed table: `stbl_sinistar_chase` for direct chase (player on-screen),
  `stbl_sinistar_orbit` when out-of-sector or approaching obliquely.
- The chase curve is **non-monotonic** — see `01-overview.md` design pillars.
  Preserve the surge-pause-surge shape.
- Orbit factor decreases over time (initial = `max_orbit_radius = 12`,
  decrement by 1 per frame, with on-screen/off-screen rules per
  `tunables.yaml#orbit_shrink_per_frame`). The orbit shrink is what makes
  Sinistar feel like it's "closing in."
- Sinistar speaks taunts (`run_coward`, `run_run_run`, `i_hunger_coward`)
  based on player behavior (fleeing? cornered?).
- On contact with player: bite (`SINIBITE`). Player dies. Sinistar continues
  hunting on next spawn.

### Damage

- Player shots **do nothing**.
- Sinibombs do 1/`pieces_required` (1/4 in original) damage per hit.
- After 4 sinibomb hits, Sinistar enters `dying`, shatters into fragments,
  and the player warps to the next sector.

### Roar

`speech.yaml#roar` plays when Sinistar passes near the player without biting,
or in critical-state escalations. It is the non-verbal counterpart to the
spoken taunts and is iconic.

## Planetoid behavior

Planetoids are not "AI" in the agentic sense, but they have a stateful loop:

```
state: idle
loop each frame:
    vibration -= vibration_damp_per_frame    (clamped at 0)
    if vibration > 0 and rand() < crystal_toss_probability:
        toss_crystal_in_random_direction(damped by crystal_toss_damping)
    if vibration >= vibration_max:
        shatter_into_fragments()
        despawn

on missile/sinibomb impact:
    vibration += missile_vibration_add
    play hit sound
    contribute to crystal-toss roll for this frame
```

Planetoid impact mechanics are independent of planetoid type; the type
affects mass (collision response) and visual size. Larger planetoids
absorb more shots before vibration reaches max.

## Swarm trigger

Periodically, the AI rolls for a "swarm" event with probability
`tunables.yaml#swarm_probability = 16/255 per 4 seconds ≈ once a minute`.
A swarm makes nearby workers and warriors converge toward a point (often
the player or a high-value planetoid), producing a sudden coordinated
attack. The swarm dissolves after a short window.

The swarm is one of the game's signature pacing mechanisms — it ensures
that even calm moments are punctuated by sudden coordinated threats.

## Modernization notes

- AI behavior should run at 60Hz fixed-step but on **slower decision
  cadences** (every 8–32 frames per AI), not on per-frame deliberation.
  This is what makes the AI feel "thoughtful" rather than twitchy.
- Speed tables are data; load from `speed-tables.yaml` and parameterize.
  This makes AI tuning a content task, not a code change.
- Preserve the *shapes* of the speed curves, especially the non-monotonic
  Sinistar chase. The numbers can scale; the curve shape is design.
- The shooting-inhibitor-only-on-screen rule is a deliberate readability
  choice. Preserve it.
- The probabilistic worker / warrior off-screen behavior is what makes
  the world feel alive without overwhelming the player. Preserve it.
